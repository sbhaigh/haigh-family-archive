Chapter I · The man

Ernest

Ernest Harmson was 21 when he was killed in action on 1 July 1916. His memorial register records him as the son of George and Esther Harmson of 118 Poppleton Road, York.

He served as Private 23855 in the 10th Battalion, West Yorkshire Regiment (Prince of Wales's Own). No identified grave bears his name. Instead, he is commemorated with the missing of the Somme at Thiepval.

Chapter II · 1 July 1916

Fricourt

At 7.30am the 10th West Yorkshires went forward north of Fricourt, close to the cratered ground known as the Tambour. Their war diary recorded four assault lines, machine-gun fire and catastrophic casualties.

The attack

The battalion belonged to 50th Brigade, 17th (Northern) Division. For the opening attack it fought on the northern edge of Fricourt alongside the 21st Division. The wider plan sought to envelop the German-held village.

The Tambour had been shaped by months of underground mine warfare. Mine charges were fired immediately before zero hour.

“The Battn assaulted in 4 lines. 2 lines got through the German Position … Casualties were very heavy.”

10th West Yorkshire war diary, transcription cited below; TNA series WO 95/2004.

07:28

Mine charges at the Tambour were fired immediately before zero hour. Sources differ over the functioning of the individual charges.

07:30

The battalion advanced in four lines. The war diary says leading lines penetrated the German position while later lines were caught by machine-gun fire across no man's land.

Morning

Fire enfiladed the battalion's left. With the attack on its left failing, men who had penetrated the German position became isolated.

Later

The battalion was withdrawn. The diary initially estimated about 750 other-rank casualties; later summaries give 22 officer casualties and 688 other ranks. Ernest was among those killed.

2 July

Fricourt was occupied after the German withdrawal from the salient. Burial parties began the work that created the cemetery now lying on this battlefield.

Chapter III · The aftermath

Fricourt New Military Cemetery

This is not simply a cemetery near the battlefield. It was made by Ernest's battalion after Fricourt was taken, and the majority of its graves belong to the 10th West Yorkshires.

210First World War burials
15910th West Yorkshire graves
26Unidentified burials

Four large graves

CWGC historical information describes the cemetery as four large graves made by the 10th West Yorkshire Regiment after the capture of Fricourt in July 1916, with a small number of later graves added.

Rows of West Yorkshire headstones make the battalion's losses tangible. Records for the cemetery include men of the 10th Battalion killed on 1 July, among them Private Ernest Stowe, Lieutenant Alfred Victor Ratcliffe, Private Cornelius Kidger and Captain Geoffrey Gaskell Blackburn.

Ernest is not here

Ernest has no identified grave. That does not establish exactly what happened to his remains: they may never have been recovered, or may have been recovered but not identified. The archive therefore avoids claiming more than the evidence supports.

His absence from the cemetery is what takes the story onwards to Thiepval.

Chapter IV · The missing

Thiepval

Ernest's story ends not with a grave, but with a name cut into stone on the Memorial to the Missing of the Somme.

Pier and Faces 2A, 2C and 2D

Your photograph records the inscription HARMSON E. The memorial register gives the fuller identity: Private Ernest Harmson, 23855, 10th Battalion, West Yorkshire Regiment, died 1 July 1916, aged 21, son of George and Esther Harmson of York.

The Thiepval Memorial commemorates more than 72,000 British and South African servicemen who died in the Somme sector and have no known grave. Ernest is one of them.
