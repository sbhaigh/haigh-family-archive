The Somme · 1 July 1916

Fricourt

The battle in which Private Ernest Harmson was killed, reconstructed around the ground, the battalion record, contemporary maps and eyewitness testimony.

The plan

Fricourt formed a German-held salient between the British attacks around La Boisselle and Mametz. The opening plan sought to envelop the village rather than storm its strongest positions head-on.

The 10th West Yorkshires attacked on the northern side of Fricourt beside the mine-cratered Tambour. At 07:28 three mines were fired there; at 07:30 the battalion advanced in four lines.

The battalion war diary records that its leading lines penetrated the German position, but the attack on the left failed and machine guns enfiladed the West Yorkshires. Later figures record 22 officer casualties and 688 other ranks.

10th West Yorkshire Regiment

Zero hour

07:28

Three mines, containing about 49,000 lb of explosive in total, were detonated at the Tambour immediately before the infantry attack.

07:30

The 10th West Yorkshires went forward in four lines. Ernest Harmson was one of the battalion's soldiers, although the surviving evidence does not yet identify his company or precise position.

Morning

Two lines reached or penetrated the German position. Machine-gun fire, especially enfilading fire against the left, devastated the later waves and isolated men who had gone forward.

14:30

Further attacks around the Fricourt salient took place. The German position continued to inflict losses before the defenders withdrew during the night.

2 July

Fricourt was occupied after the German withdrawal. The battlefield then became a place of recovery and burial.

Reading the ground

What the map explains

The Tambour

Mine-cratered ground immediately beside the battalion’s attack. Three charges were fired at 07:28, two minutes before zero.

Enfilade fire

The battalion diary records machine guns firing into the left flank. Cambridge’s Sassoon research identifies Wing Corner as a particularly destructive machine-gun position.

Four lines

The 10th West Yorkshires advanced in four assault lines. The leading lines penetrated the German position; later waves were devastated crossing No Man’s Land.

Sassoon on the ridge

Lieutenant Siegfried Sassoon of the 1st Royal Welch Fusiliers watched from Crawley Ridge, about 500 yards behind the front. Cambridge University Library identifies the attack he described with men “cheering as if at a football match” as the 10th West Yorkshires advancing on his left.

From battlefield to memorial

Fricourt New Military Cemetery was made by the 10th West Yorkshires after the village was captured. Ernest has no identified grave, so his story continues at Thiepval.
